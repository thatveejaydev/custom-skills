# Joke Library

Rules:
- One beat every **3–5 sentences** (half that for serious topics).
- **≤ 12 words** per joke, placed **immediately after** the fact it reacts to.
- Setup = the fact itself (already in the script). Payoff = the short line after it. Never spend a sentence purely setting up a joke.
- Punch **up or sideways** (rulers, companies, systems, myths, bad decisions, the narrator's own past self), never at victims, the audience or vulnerable groups.
- A joke may not introduce a new fact. If it needs one the user didn't provide, cut it.

---

## 1. Deadpan understatement after something horrific
Describe the horror accurately, then grade it absurdly mildly.
- "Technically, he kept his word, but you know, that's not great."
- "Yeah, it's not looking too hot for Ivan here."
- "The direct hits, panic, and freezing Baltic seawater were a bad combination."
- "Not sure how that would work, but the enthusiasm was there."
- "Not only was this an extremely cruel plan, but it didn't work most of the time."
- "kind of evil, right"

**Formula:** `[Brutal fact]. [Mild-grade verdict: "that's not great" / "bad combination" / "not looking too hot"].`

## 2. Anachronistic / corporate framing
Describe a historical horror in modern business, tech or lifestyle language.
- "This opened up a huge market opportunity for creative engineers."
- "Stalin invented the next great job opportunity for expendable soldiers, the human mine clearer. The job description was simple."
- "The devices were big and bulky and couldn't be used on the go."
- "So, instead of paying them back, he came up with a slightly cheaper solution. Get rid of all of them."
- "It's like a medieval Swiss pocket knife."
- "That's a lot of taxpayer money." (instead of calling in an air strike)

**Formula:** `[Historical actor] + [startup / job ad / product review / budget vocabulary].`

## 3. Sarcastic one-word or short verdict
A standalone reaction line, often the script's final sting.
- "Congratulations."
- "Nice."
- "Let's go."
- "Yeah, maybe later." (on climbing 1,665 stairs)
- "Oh, hell no."
- "Okay, then."
- "Yeah, a bit too late there, buddy."
- "And you know what? I like it."

**Formula:** `[Consequence fact]. [1–4 word reaction].`

## 4. Pun / name aside
A relative clause that jokes on a name or title.
- "Pope Innocent IV, who wasn't particularly innocent, …"
- "Guy Fawkes, who was a guy."
- "…named it Fanta, which means imagination, kind of, in German."
- "…the Timurid Empire, named after himself, of course, because why not?"
- "a llama which they very creatively called Napoleon"

**Formula:** `[Name], who [plays on the name], [continues sentence].`

## 5. Literalized idiom
Use an idiom, then point out it was literally true.
- "they were so hungry that they could eat a horse, literally."
- "loaded his ashes into a cannon, and fired him back towards Poland."
- "turn those pesky balloons into beautiful flammable fireworks."
- "you would turn into a glorified ceiling fan." (strappado)
- "you can literally stand in America, look at Russia, and see tomorrow."

**Formula:** `[Idiom that happens to be physically true] + "literally".`

## 6. Absurd specific / deflating list item
The last item in a list deflates the rest.
- "an army… consisting out of 600 men, a singular brick, and a 100 horses."
- "Inside they kept politicians, defectors, and worst of all, tennis players."
- "The Soviets, NATO, Yugoslavia, take your pick."
- "On beaches, in mountains, in cities, next to people's homes. Okay, maybe not everywhere."

**Formula:** `[Serious item], [serious item], and [absurd item].` / `[Exaggeration]. Okay, maybe not [exaggeration].`

## 7. Edgy topical jab (usually the closer)
Turn the story into a dig at a modern power. Only use it if the reference is universally known AND doesn't assert a new specific fact; otherwise keep it vague.
- "But don't ask what the US did between 2001 and 2009."
- "It's not the US and Mexico, just yet."
- "Also, you get killed for questioning Stalin."
- "So, they might as well have been your drug dealer." (Coca-Cola)
- "the CIA… I mean a Marxist" (fake slip, Group B)
- "If the Nazis hadn't opened the gas chambers, maybe the sympathy would have lasted."

## 8. Mock dialogue from historical actors
Paraphrased modern speech. Keep it as short as a tweet.
- "Until one guy was like, 'Why don't we just put an anti-mine attachment on our tanks?'"
- "'No problem,' said Stalin, 'we have more bodies than the Germans have mines.'"
- "'Hi, we surrender. Please save my horses.'"
- "'Wait, what?' said Guy Fawkes… 'I'm not going to let that happen.'"
- "the authorities were like, 'You know what? Let's make Protestantism the main state religion.'"
- "that were like, 'Hey, that's free real estate.'"
- "during that time she was like, well, might as well make the best out of this situation"

**Formula:** `[Actor] was like, "[1-sentence modern paraphrase of their real decision]."`

## 9. Viewer-directed aside / fourth wall
- "Guys, stop trying to." (swimming through tsunamis)
- "Sorry, ladies."
- "There are pictures online if you want to see it."
- "Yeah, you are laughing, but…"
- "and you might already know what I'm going to say here"
- "Everyone knows what D-Day is. I hope."
- "and I wish I was joking"
- "I apologize, but no."

## 10. Gamer / internet slang applied to the topic
- "but that's just a skill issue" (dodos)
- "The Medieval meta weapon" / "squishy target" / "armor ratings"
- "one-v-one-ing their political opponent"
- "your homies from kindergarten"
- "as esteemed gym bros"
- "pulling an emperor Nero"
- "NATO was bombing the absolute crisps out of them"

## 11. Callback / planted payoff
Plant early, pay off near the end with a short line.
- Plant: "…and worst of all, tennis players. Remember them, they are important to the story later." → Payoff: "But then tennis time."
- Plant: "If Hitler wanted the view, he would have to climb 1,665 stairs. Yeah, maybe later." → Payoff: "Then when later came though, …"
- Plant: "life was essentially a coin flip" → Payoff: "like being a gambler but… you quite literally don't have the luxury to stop gambling."

## 12. Ironic juxtaposition (good thing, bad reason)
- "though it was obviously a tactic to prevent them from rebelling."
- "Well, it's kind of hard to feed people state propaganda if the people can't read."
- "And the French king, well, he didn't have to pay back his loan anymore."
- "And to really rub some salt in the wound, the government actually paid the brewery a refund on their taxes."
- "so the responsible people they were, they immediately got married"

## 13. Debatable first-person take (comment driver, 1 per script)
A clear opinion the viewer can disagree with. It's the narrator's judgment, never a factual claim.
- "My personal pick though goes to Timur the Great…" *(creator)*
- "And you know what? I like it." *(creator, about duelling politicians)*
- "for me personally it has to be Pol Pot" *(creator, Group B)*
- "Honestly? Kind of genius."
- "Worst plan in history. I respect it."
- "I'd take the sniper over the paperwork, personally."

Place it near a judgment the input already implies: the most cruel, the best, the dumbest, the smartest.

## Punch-up pass (run after the draft)
1. Rank every joke beat in the draft from weakest to strongest. A weak joke is generic (it would fit any topic), long (> 12 words), explains itself, or repeats a type already used.
2. For the 2 weakest, write 3 alternatives, each from a different type above.
3. Pick the one that is **shortest**, **most specific to this fact**, and **most surprising**. If none beats "no joke", cut it.

Example (password script):
- Weak: "And hackers love you for it." (generic)
- Alt A (anachronism): "Basically, you've given hackers a master key with a free trial."
- Alt B (understatement): "Which is, technically, very generous of you."
- Alt C (verdict): "Bold strategy."
- **Keep B:** specific to "reusing" = "giving", deadpan, 8 words.

---

## Variety rules
- Patterns, not lines: never paste a creator sentence verbatim beyond 4 words, except stock phrases ("hear me out", "Congratulations.", "Nice.", "Let's go.", "Thing is,", "Subscribe.").
- Each signature phrase at most once per script.
- Use at least 3 different joke types per script, and never the same type twice in a row.
- Across consecutive scripts, rotate the sting type (verdict → jab → gut-punch → callback).

---

## Serious-mode jokes (restrained)
For death-heavy or otherwise serious topics (illness, grief, abuse, safety, money loss), use only types 1, 9 and 12, and at most 2–3 per script. Let the final line be a gut-punch irony:
- "So, the leaders were forced to look directly at what they had done."
- "And the worst part, just 3 days later, the reinforcements finally arrived."
- "or the other name which I can't say"
- "unfortunately Dazai succeeded where his imagination failed"
