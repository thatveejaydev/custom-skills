# Style Guide

Distilled from 60 transcripts: 40 later "Group A" shorts (`#4k`/`#shorts`, the core voice) and 20 earlier "Group B" shorts (`#animation`, where only selected devices are kept, see §11).

The source creator makes history shorts, so the quoted examples are history. **Every rule applies to any topic.** §13 shows how to translate each pattern to tech, finance, fitness, business, product, personal-story and explainer scripts.

Measured on Group A:
- Delivery speed: **3.1 words/sec** (range 2.7–3.9). 10 seconds ≈ 31 words.
- Length: 125–313 words (40–110 s), with a typical length of ~230 words.
- Sentence length: **average 12.9, median 12 words** across all sentences. Per script, the middle 50% have a median of 11–15 words and 12–27% fragments (≤ 6 words); the longest sentence is usually 23–35 words.
- Most frequent connective words: "So" (~88 uses), "But" (~82), "basically" (15), "actually" (18), "literally" (13), "Well," (16).

---

## 1. Narrator persona

- A casual, slightly cheeky narrator who is amused by how stupid, weird, cruel or surprising the topic is, and tells it like a story to a friend. (The source creator is Dutch and self-references it: "being a Dutchman myself I know all about the protein gains". Use identity self-inserts only with facts about the user that you actually know.)
- **Point of view:** second-person "you" by default. "We / us / people" for people in general. "I / me / my" for opinions and asides.
  - "If you really hate someone, you can just shoot them in the past."
  - "My personal pick though goes to Timur the Great…"
  - "And you know what? I like it."
- **Tone:** deadpan irony on top of real facts. Never mock victims or the audience; mock the decision-makers, the system, the myth, the absurdity, or the narrator.
- **Vocabulary level:** a 12–14-year-old could follow every word. Technical terms get introduced plainly ("the strappado, where you would turn into a glorified ceiling fan").
- **Slang and internet/gamer register** (use 1–3 per script, never forced):
  `skill issue` · `meta` · `one-v-one-ing` · `free real estate` · `homies` · `Let's go.` · `gym bros` · `protein gains` · `beef` · `chill` · `douchebag` · `squishy target` · `armor ratings` · `toys to choose from` · `cool clubs` · `pulling an emperor Nero` · `chaps` · `stinky trenches` · `pesky balloons` · `goofy little fellas` · `Oh, hell no.` · `Yeah, a bit too late there, buddy.`
- **Recurring phrases:** "basically", "literally", "actually", "aka", "a whopping", "Thing is,", "Hear me out.", "Long story short,", "And I kid you not,", "and I wish I was joking,", "Nazis being Nazis", "if you like to call it that", "quote unquote".

## 2. Hooks (see hook-library.md for the full library)

- 1–2 sentences, **each under 20 words**. Most real hooks are 8–18 words.
- **Curiosity trigger within the first 8 words** (≈ 2.5 s, the swipe decision). The full premise is clear within **the first 30 words**. No setup clause first.
- The hook states the *most surprising, counterintuitive or high-stakes point*, not the topic name. ("Russia was once invaded by an 8-year-old boy who had already been dead for 13 years.", not "Today: the False Dmitry.")
- Followed immediately by a **validation beat** in ~70% of scripts: "What? Yes, that sounds like complete nonsense." / "Yeah, you are laughing, but this really happened." / "Hear me out." / "Let me explain."

## 3. Structure and flow

Standard escalation skeleton (variants in structure-templates.md):

| % of script | Beat | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10% | Hook + validation | "Imagine dying to a literal tsunami of beer. Yeah, you are laughing, but this really happened." |
| 10–25% | Context with a concrete anchor from the input (year, place, number, name, product, specific situation) | "In 1814, one of the biggest breweries in London had this gigantic 7 m tall wooden vat…" |
| 20–35% | The problem / dilemma | "The Catholic Church had a little problem." / "Kamarov had to make a choice." |
| 30–70% | Escalation: 2–4 steps, each worse | "First the women, then the elderly men and children." |
| 55–75% | **Re-hook** + second-biggest reveal | "But here's the craziest part." |
| 85–95% | Twist / worst part / consequence | "And the worst part, just 3 days later, the reinforcements finally arrived." |
| last 1–2 lines | Sting + CTA | "Congratulations." / "Subscribe." |

**Transitions** are 1–3 words: "But," · "So," · "Well," · "Thing is," · "Then came…" · "Fast forward to…" · "Meanwhile," · "Finally," · "Okay, but…" · "Okay, then." · "Next up," · "And then things got even weirder."

**Retention ladder** (one pull-forward every ~20 seconds):
- **Hook question:** the hook raises one question. Advance it; the core answer lands at ≥ 50% and the final twist at ≥ 60%. The sting calls back to it.
- **Open loop in the first 20%:** a teased detail, number or outcome, paid off after the main re-hook.
- **Micro-hooks every 60–80 words:** "And it gets worse." / "Thing is, that was the easy problem."
- **Main re-hook at 55–75%**, before the second-strongest point.

**Open loops / planted payoffs:** plant a detail and say you'll pay it off:
"…and worst of all, tennis players. Remember them, they are important to the story later." … "But then tennis time."

**Problem → solution framing** is the default for "how/why" topics: "So, what can you do?" / "What do we do now?" / "So, his solution was simple."

## 4. Pacing and rhythm

- **Default sentence: 8–15 words.** Then break the rhythm with a fragment or one-liner every 3–5 sentences:
  - "Easy task. Just run across, right? Wrong."
  - "Case closed until 12 years later, …"
  - "Suspicious extremely, but…"
  - "It didn't work."
  - "Okay, maybe not everywhere."
- **Speed up** in lists and escalations by using comma-chains and dropping verbs: "On beaches, in mountains, in cities, next to people's homes."
- **Slow down** for serious beats and countdowns with short declarative sentences: "The old man squints. He is confused. Five."
- **Do not over-fragment.** The creator's median is 11–12 words; only ~1 in 5 sentences is a fragment. Most sentences are relaxed, comma-joined spoken lines: "Since you are the king, everyone kind of just wants to kill you." / "The taster could eat the food, feel completely fine, and then hours later drop dead." Merge adjacent fragments unless they form a deliberate beat ("Easy task. Just run across, right? Wrong.").
- A point runs **2–4 sentences** max, then pivots.
- **Rhetorical Q → instant A** (≈ 1.3 questions per script): "But why would he do that? Well, it's kind of hard to feed people state propaganda if the people can't read."

## 5. Humor (see joke-library.md)

- Frequency: **one beat every 3–5 sentences**, which is ~4–8 per 230-word script. Halve that for serious topics.
- Placement: **directly after the fact** it comments on, as a separate short sentence or a trailing clause. Never set up a joke over multiple sentences.
- Main types, in order of frequency: deadpan understatement/irony · anachronistic modern framing · sarcastic one-word verdicts · literalized idioms · pun/name asides · absurd specifics · edgy topical jabs · viewer-directed asides.
- Edgy/dark jokes are in-voice (Nazi jabs, CIA, US torture, "you get killed for questioning Stalin"). Target is always power, systems or bad decisions, never victims.

## 6. Storytelling devices

- **One protagonist** (or "one guy") per story. Introduce with a plain role: "One guy, commissioned by the king, who knew potatoes were actually a superfood…"
- **Dilemmas:** "Walk away, let the SS do their thing, but bear the guilt or fight for the lives of the innocent. He chose the latter."
- **Modern analogies:** "like a medieval Swiss pocket knife" · "Think like Dunkirk, but then for the Nazis and three times bigger" · "concrete mushrooms".
- **Pop-culture bridges:** "Order 66 in Star Wars is actually based on a real-life event."
- **Personification of nations/institutions:** "Greece and Turkey had beef over religion", "team pope / team no pope".
- **Myth-busting:** "Contrary to popular belief, …" / "And no, [X]. That one is a postwar myth."
- **Thought experiments:** "Let's say someone kills a person in the Netherlands and flees into this small part of Belgium. Does that mean…?"

## 7. Emotional arc

- Energy starts **high** (hook) → **steady climb** (each beat worse/weirder) → **peak at 75–90%** (worst part / twist) → **drop** to a dry, quiet sting.
- Comedic topics: the peak is the most absurd fact; the sting is a sarcastic verdict.
- Serious topics: jokes thin out as the stakes rise. The peak is the cruelest fact; the sting is a gut-punch irony, not a gag.

## 8. Information delivery

- **Every number gets an anchor:** a year, a comparison, or a per-unit breakdown.
  - "300,000 innocent people were killed in just 6 weeks. That's 8,300 people per day."
  - "1.5 million L of beer… That is the weight of about a thousand cars."
  - "a jet engine generates about 140 decibels… Krakatoa wasn't just twice as loud, it was actually trillions of times more powerful."
  - Only do arithmetic on numbers the user supplied. Never introduce new statistics.
- **Sources:** never cite formally in-voice. If the input names a source the claim depends on (a study, a law, a company statement), keep a short spoken attribution ("according to [source]"). Light attribution only where the fact is uncertain: "Historians estimate…" · "One estimate says…" · "reportedly" · "Legend has it…" · "most sources suggest…".
- **Simplify by mechanism:** explain *why it worked* in one plain sentence: "Musketss back then were wildly inaccurate. You didn't aim. You just pointed in the general direction of the enemy and wished for the best."
- **Explain:Entertain ratio** is about 70:30. Every joke rides on a fact; no joke-only stretches longer than one sentence.

## 9. Endings and CTA

- The **last content line is a sting** (≤ 15 words): irony, sarcastic verdict, edgy jab, or gut-punch.
  - "And the French king, well, he didn't have to pay back his loan anymore. Congratulations."
  - "Luckily, his own generals just didn't. Nice."
  - "But don't ask what the US did between 2001 and 2009."
  - "You rig the vote and you've become the next pope. Let's go."
  - "Yeah, a bit too late there, buddy. Also, you get killed for questioning Stalin."
- **CTA:** one bare word or phrase on its own, as the final line. Pick by platform:
  - YouTube / Shorts / no platform named: `Subscribe.` (creator default) · `Subscribe for more.`
  - TikTok / Instagram Reels / Facebook / X: `Follow for more.` · `Follow.`
  - LinkedIn: `Follow for more.`
  - The user's own CTA (a link, product, newsletter, "comment X"): keep it, compressed to ≤ 10 words in the same bare style ("Link's in the bio."). Never add an offer, discount or link that isn't in the input.
- **Series tease (optional):** only if the user's material covers a follow-up: "Next short we will talk about the other time they fought together. So subscribe."
- Never: recap, moral, "thanks for watching", "let me know in the comments", or ending on an open question.

## 10. Formatting of the delivered script

Output is **pure narration** (the user's choice). The creator's scripts often imply visuals ("these guys or these funny guys?", "this map", "from this to this"). **Do not** write those deictic references unless the user's script explicitly mentions a visual. Without the footage they don't make sense spoken aloud. Write paragraph breaks only as breath breaks (one per beat). Never use brackets, stage directions or markup.

## 11. Kept from Group B (optional tools; use Group A rhythm)

| Device | Group B example | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| **Countdown list** ("Number three… Number two… Number one…") | "three of the strangest pets ever kept in history number three Napoleon the war Llama…" | Topic is a list/ranking. Save the best for #1. |
| **"Meet [X]" introduction** | "meet the first tsar of Russia" / "meet Wilbur and Orville" | Introducing the protagonist after the hook. |
| **Arc-in-a-line hook** | "from prostitute to prisoner to wife to pirate lord the life of… Ching Shih was a wild one" | Biography with a dramatic rise or fall. |
| **Life-stage beats** ("Age six, … Age 18, …") | Frida Kahlo: "age six she got polio… age 18 riding the bus home she got into a horrible accident" | Biography; it gives a ticking structure. |
| **"How would life be if…" / day-in-the-life** | "how would life be if we lived during the peak of the black plague… life was essentially a coin flip" | "Daily life in X" topics; extend one metaphor throughout. |
| **Extended metaphor payoff** | "living during the black plague was like being a gambler but… you quite literally don't have the luxury to stop gambling" | One metaphor introduced early, paid off at the end. |
| **"You tell me" verdict** | "so you tell me did he really earn the title of the Terrible" | Debatable-title topics. Use it as the sting, not as an engagement question. |
| **Twist-word ending** | Jesters: "the colors faded, the smiles disappeared, and the titles changed… the politician" | A reveal of what something became. |
| **Self-insert nationality joke** | "being a Dutchman myself I know all about the protein gains" | Only if the user's own nationality/identity is relevant and known. |
| **Restrained serious mode** | Hiroshima countdown, Soyuz | Death-heavy or sensitive topics: fewer jokes, more short sentences. |

**Do NOT keep from Group B:** encyclopedic phrasing ("a raw and brutal journey to the depths of existential despair", "grappled with personal demons", "showcased his tactical brilliance", "a relentless cycle of suffering and survival") · adjective stacks · run-on sentences · no "you" · endings like "what do you think" or "was this one of the best marketing campaigns ever" · self-promo endings ("why don't you watch our latest YouTube video").

## 12. Anti-patterns: never do these

- Greetings, channel names, "in today's video", "let's dive in", "before we start".
- Taking more than 30 words to reach the premise.
- Recaps, morals, "and that's why this matters", "so remember…".
- Hedge stacks ("it is possible that perhaps"), academic connectors ("furthermore", "moreover", "notably", "additionally", "in conclusion", "it is worth noting").
- AI-flavoured words: "delve", "tapestry", "testament", "intricate", "pivotal", "multifaceted", "a stark reminder", "embark", "realm", "unravel".
- Two storylines in one script, or a beat longer than 4 sentences.
- Jokes that punch at victims or the audience, or jokes that require facts not in the source.
- Ending with a question to the viewer.
- Stage directions, visual cues or hashtags in the script.
- Social-media filler: "Hey guys", "Welcome back", "Make sure to like and subscribe" up front, "Let me know in the comments", "Stay tuned", "Without further ado", "Game-changer", "Hack" used as a filler noun, "You won't believe…".

## 13. Adapting to any topic

The patterns transfer; the subject matter doesn't. Translate like this:

| Pattern (history original) | Tech / product | Finance / business | Fitness / health | Personal story / opinion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Villain/decision-maker to mock (Stalin, the Pope) | The company, the default setting, the old way | The bank, the fee structure, "Past You" | The fad, the myth, the gym-bro advice | Past self, the situation, the system |
| Year anchor ("In 1814…") | Version/launch/date from input | Amount, rate or date from input | Duration, weight, rep count from input | When/where it happened, from input |
| Escalation ladder (each atrocity worse) | Each problem/feature more surprising | Each cost/gain bigger | Each mistake costlier, each fix more effective | Each step of the story higher-stakes |
| Re-hook ("But here's the craziest part") | "But here's the part nobody uses." | "But here's where it gets expensive." | "But here's the part that actually matters." | "And then things got even weirder." |
| Anachronistic framing ("a market opportunity for creative engineers") | Describe software like a medieval tool, or a bug like a feature | Describe a fee like a subscription you never signed up for | Describe soreness like a customer review | Describe life events like a patch note / job description |
| Sting ("Congratulations.") | Ironic verdict on the product/default | Ironic verdict on the money outcome | Ironic verdict on the effort | Ironic verdict on the narrator |

Rules for non-history scripts:
- **Persuasive / promo scripts:** the hook is the viewer's problem or the most surprising benefit *as stated in the input*. Never inflate claims ("the best", "guaranteed", "in 7 days") beyond the input's own wording.
- **Tutorials / tips:** use Template B (problem→solution) or C (list). Each tip = action + why it works + one joke. Keep steps in the input's order if order matters.
- **Personal stories:** keep first-person "I" for the narrator's events, and use "you" for the viewer's parallel ("If you've ever…").
- **Edgy jabs** stay in-voice, but aim them at the topic's own systems, myths or bad decisions. Don't jab at real named companies or people unless the input already criticizes them.
- **Niche jargon:** keep the terms the audience needs, explain each in ≤ 8 plain words the first time, and cut the rest.
