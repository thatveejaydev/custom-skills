# Hook Library

Rules: 1–2 sentences, each < 20 words. **Curiosity trigger inside the first 8 words**, full premise inside the first 30. Use a hook tournament: 5 drafts of different types, scored, best wins (see SKILL.md step 3). Lead with the **weirdest/darkest fact**, not the topic. In most cases, follow with a validation beat (bottom of this file).

Pick the hook type by asking which one makes the user's single most surprising point land hardest.

The quoted examples are the creator's (history). The templates work for any topic. Below are illustrative translations, written for this skill rather than taken from the creator; facts in a real rewrite must come from the user's input:

## 1. Absurd bold claim
State the strangest true fact flatly, as if it's normal.
- "Russia was once invaded by an 8-year-old boy who had already been dead for 13 years." *(16 words)*
- "French people hate potatoes, or at least they used to." *(10)*
- "Belgium is completely surrounded by the Netherlands. Well, this part of it is." *(13, self-correcting)*
- "The Zeppelin is the worst thing to happen in the history of warfare." *(13)*
- "In the year 1252, humanity officially devolved by making torturing mainstream. And somehow this has to do with Jesus." *(19)*
- "America has some really weird borders." *(6)*

**Template:** `[Familiar thing] [did/was impossible-sounding thing]. [Optional: qualifier that makes it weirder.]`

## 2. Accusation / you-are-implicated
Point the fact at the viewer or someone they know.
- "If your friend likes Fanta, he is probably a Nazi or at least he would have been in 1940." *(18)*
- "If you really hate someone, you can just shoot them in the past. No, not now. Not that." *(18)*

**Template:** `If you/your friend [everyday thing], [shocking historical implication]. [Clarifying walk-back.]`

## 3. Imagine / let's-say (viewer inside the scene)
- "Imagine dying to a literal tsunami of beer." *(8)*
- "Imagine this. It's World War I and you are tasked to plant a bomb on the other side of the street." *(20)*
- "So, let's say you're strolling through ancient Rome and suddenly get the urge to cut down a tree…" *(18)*
- *(Group B)* "imagine yourself standing on a battlefield heroically fighting off invaders and then your arm is gone" *(16; the twist ending inside the hook)*

**Template:** `Imagine [ordinary action] [absurd/brutal outcome].`

## 4. Cold open (mid-scene, often with fake dialogue)
Drop into the moment, then zoom out.
- "Dang, the bully is coming again. Quick, choose your weapon." *(10)*
- "60 seconds before the nuke detonates. An old couple in Hiroshima is finishing breakfast." *(14; countdown structure)*
- "Manfa, the allies are here. What are we going to do? Hello. Kill all the horses." *(16)*
- *(Group B)* "dudes, morning Siberia, what a beautiful day to be alive" *(before a massive explosion)*

**Template:** `[Line of dialogue or sensory moment]. [Line that reveals the stakes.]`

## 5. Misconception flip ("Nope, wrong.")
Voice the common belief, then kill it.
- "Well, at least this will go down in history as the most famous and deadliest sea disaster. Nope, wrong." *(19)*
- "If you had to guess which country in the world has the most bunkers, you would probably say Russia or the US. But, it's neither." *(guess + flip)*
- "Everyone knows what D-Day is. I hope. But do you know about the fake D-Day that happened?"
- "For the 1900's World Fair… how did the French manage to keep such a massive structure a secret…? Turns out they didn't." *(fake question, deflated)*

**Template:** `[Common belief / guess]. [Nope, wrong. / But, it's neither. / Turns out they didn't.]`

## 6. Pop-culture / modern bridge
- "Order 66 in Star Wars is actually based on a real-life event. Hear me out." *(15)*
- "Nowadays, Japan is kawaii and a tourist hot spot. But do you know about their evil and simply demonic recent history?"
- "This mask, surely you know it, but where is it from? And why does it have to do with blowing up the government?"
- "If you have ever been to London, you have surely seen these goofy little fellas."

**Template:** `[Thing everyone knows today] is actually [dark historical origin]. Hear me out.`

## 7. Direct question with a twist built in
Only works if the question itself is strange. Answer within 2 sentences.
- "What happens if a kamikaze pilot failed?" *(7)*
- "Who should own Greenland? Denmark, America? Well, according to history, there are actually seven different groups…"
- "Why don't they just unite and become a strong country together? Well, because they absolutely hate each other…"
- "Throughout our history, there are many, and I mean many people, nicknamed the great. But… who was the worst the great person?"

**Template:** `[Strange question]? [Well, + blunt answer that raises a bigger question.]`

## 8. Tutorial / how-to parody
- "Here's how to guarantee to become a pope in about 55 years. First, you have to be born a man. So, that eliminates about 50% of your chances. Sorry, ladies."

**Template:** `Here's how to [absurd goal] in [timeframe]. First, [step]. [Joke.]`

## 9. Setup-the-normal, then puncture
- "Empires are generally really big. Definitely bigger than, I don't know, London, for example. But what if I told you there was once an empire so tiny it could fit seven times in London?"
- "World War I was one of the deadliest conflicts the world has ever seen. But in reality, it was one big family argument…"
- "World War II, basically Nazis versus allies. But did you know there were two instances where it was Nazis and allies versus other Nazis?"
- "During a siege, you're almost guaranteed to starve to death unless your general finds a brand new meat source. Oh, hell no."

**Template:** `[Obvious general truth]. But [the exception that breaks it].`

## 10. Arc-in-a-line (Group B, biographies)
- "from prostitute to prisoner to wife to pirate lord, the life of Ching Shih was a wild one"
- "there are many people in history that earned the nickname of the great but there is only one who earned the title of the terrible"

**Template:** `From [A] to [B] to [C] to [D], the life of [name] was a wild one.`

## The 8-word test
Read only the first 8 words. Would a stranger stop scrolling?

| Fails (trigger arrives late) | Passes (trigger in ≤ 8 words) |
|---|---|
| "According to a 2019 survey by Google, 65% of people reuse passwords…" | "Two out of three people make hackers' lives easy." |
| "In 1814, one of the biggest breweries in London had a vat…" | "Imagine dying to a literal tsunami of beer." *(creator)* |
| "Throughout history, monarchs faced many threats, and poison was…" | "The most common way to kill a king wasn't a sword." |
| "So today I want to talk about why Albania has bunkers…" | "This tiny country has half a million bunkers." |

Creator hooks that pass: "Russia was once invaded by an 8-year-old boy…" (trigger: *invaded by an 8-year-old*) · "If your friend likes Fanta, he is probably a Nazi…" (trigger by word 8) · "French people hate potatoes…" (word 4).

## Open loops (plant in the first 20%, pay off late)
Pattern examples:
- **Teased detail:** "…and worst of all, tennis players. Remember them, they are important to the story later." *(creator)*
- **Teased number:** "Spoiler: the final number is the worst part."
- **Teased outcome:** "It worked. Just not the way anyone planned."
- **Teased twist:** "And the guy who saved them? That's the weird part."
- **Ranked list:** "…and number one is honestly a bit embarrassing."

## Micro-hooks (every 60–80 words)
"But that's not even the weird part." · "And it gets worse." · "Thing is, that was the easy problem." · "Okay, but that doesn't answer the real question." *(creator)* · "And then things got even weirder." *(creator)* · "Not so fast." · "Here's the catch."

## Validation beats (sentence 2 or 3)
Use one immediately after the hook in ~70% of scripts:
- "What? Yes, that sounds like complete nonsense. But…"
- "Yeah, you are laughing, but this really happened."
- "I know, sounds weird, but hear me out."
- "Hear me out." / "Let me explain."
- "It almost sounds too stupid to believe, but…"
- "Yes, [restate the claim flatly]." ("Yes, Fanta was a Nazi drink.")
- "Oh, hell no." (for gross/dark premises)
- "And this might surprise you, but…"

## Weak hooks (rewrite these on sight)
- "Have you ever wondered about…" / "Have you ever noticed that…"
- "In this video we'll look at…"
- "[Name], a prominent [role], lived a [adjective] life marked by…"
- "Throughout history, many people have…" with no twist in the same sentence.
- "In [year], amidst the turmoil of…" as the first words, with no surprising fact.
